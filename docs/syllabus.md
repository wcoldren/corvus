# Latin Syllabus

## How the course works

About 80 daily lessons of roughly 10 minutes take you from zero to reading Aquinas and Seneca with a dictionary. At about one lesson every 1.5 days, which leaves room for missed days and review days, that is about four months.

- **Each lesson:** one new idea, a short story passage, 3–5 exercises, and one card file of about 13–14 new words and 20–22 cards in all (the budget is in `docs/card-authoring.md`).
- **Pronunciation:** Ecclesiastical throughout (*c* before e/i = "ch", *g* before e/i = "j", *ae* = "eh", *ti* + vowel = "tsee").
- **Review rhythm:** a quick quiz on older material every 5th lesson, and a full review lesson at the end of each unit.
- **Chats:** one chat per lesson in this project. Open each by pasting the previous lesson's recap.
- **Flashcards:** daily reviews in the app take care of memorization, so lessons can stay short and keep moving. Reviews take about 20 minutes a day, rarely more than 30.
- **Vocabulary:** about 1,100 words by the end of the course, or nearer 900 if review and reading lessons add none. Both figures come from a simulation with guessed answer times, and get rechecked after 30 days of logged reviews.
- **Pace rule:** a missed day changes nothing. When the app suggests a review day, take it before the next new lesson.

## The story: *Fabula Felicis*

Felix, a novice apothecary at the mountain abbey of *Mons Ferri*, discovers that the old alchemical formulas really work, but only when spoken in flawless Latin. The fantasy rule doubles as the grammar lesson: say *aquam* where *aqua* belongs and the flask boils over.

- **Felix** (*felix*, "lucky"): eager, careless with endings, learning exactly what you are learning.
- **Frater Anselmus:** the old master apothecary; speaks slowly and correctly, then later in long scholastic sentences.
- **Corvus:** a raven who talks only in Latin, mocks bad grammar, and quotes Seneca at bad moments.
- **The scriptorium:** where Felix copies Cicero and Seneca by day, bringing in classical Latin.
- **The Office:** the sung prayers at fixed hours, bringing in Church Latin.

Passages start at 2–3 sentences and grow with each unit. The fantasy stays light: the magic is a reason for correct grammar to matter, never a replacement for the real texts we read later.

## Lesson plan

Seven units of 10–14 lessons; every unit ends in a review lesson and a real text.

The **#** column is a topic's place in the plan, and **Cards** names the card file that taught it. The two numbers differ because the first chats each covered more than one row: `lesson-02` covers rows 2–4. A card file keeps the number of the chat that wrote it and is never renumbered or moved, since its card ids carry that number, so this column is the only map between the two.

A blank Cards cell means the topic hasn't been taught yet, and the next lesson takes the first blank row. Fill in the cell when the card file is committed. Some rows are partly started. For rows 5 and 6, *est* and *sunt* (`lesson-02`), *amat, ōrā, labōrā* (`lesson-01`) and *amant* (`lesson-03`) already have vocab cards. Row 7's *-er* nouns (*puer, ager*) were left for later.

## Flashcard app

The app, **corvus**, is built in this repo, and `SPEC.md` describes it. It is a web app on GitHub Pages, added to the iPad Home Screen and scheduled with FSRS (the algorithm modern Anki uses).

- **Card files:** each lesson ends with one, `cards/lesson-NN.json`, written to `docs/card-authoring.md` and committed to the repo. The file carries its lesson number and unit, so quizzes can draw on older material. Its cards join the reviews once the lesson is unlocked in the app.
- **Card types:**
  1. Vocab: Latin → English, with genitive and gender on nouns, and English → Latin for the words worth producing
  2. Concept: "What does the dative do?"
  3. Ending cells, typed: "1st decl. · genitive plural of *anima*"
  4. Parsing, on a grid: "*aquārum* → case and number"
  5. Headwords, typed: "*hominem* → *homō*", the skill of reading with a dictionary
  6. Phrases: a short sentence to translate
  7. Cloze, typed: a sentence from a text with one ending blanked
- **Saving progress:** review history lives on the iPad, and an export is the backup; the app reminds you after a week without one. There is no cloud sync.
